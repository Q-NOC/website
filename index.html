about

What is Q-NOC?

Q-NOC is a student research group at the Faculty of Physics, University of Warsaw, exploring unconventional approaches to computation inspired by biological neural systems and dynamical processes.

Reservoir Computing

Dynamic systems as computational substrates.

Neuromorphic Computing

Computing architectures inspired by biological neural systems.

Spiking Neural Networks

Information processing through temporal spike-based dynamics.

Physical Intelligence

Exploring computation beyond conventional digital architectures.

research

Research

Eight directions we work along. They overlap on purpose — most of what we build sits between two of them.

01

Reservoir Computing

Training only a readout on top of a rich, untrained recurrent substrate.

02

Spiking Neural Networks

Networks that carry information in spike timing rather than continuous activations.

03

Neuromorphic Hardware

Chips and circuits where memory and computation share the same physical place.

04

Edge AI

Inference under hard power, latency and memory budgets, away from the data centre.

05

Dynamical Systems

Attractors, bifurcations and the edge of chaos as a lens on what a network can compute.

06

Brain-Inspired Computing

Borrowing principles from neuroscience — plasticity, sparsity, locality — not just its vocabulary.

07

Analog / Mixed-Signal Computing

Letting physics do the arithmetic, with digital logic only where it earns its power budget.

08

Event-Based Processing

Sensors and pipelines that produce data only when something actually changes.

In reservoir computing the recurrent layer stays fixed. An input signal perturbs a complex, high-dimensional dynamical system, and the resulting trajectory x(t) becomes the computational space — a rich, nonlinear, fading-memory representation of everything the reservoir has recently seen. Only the readout is trained: it learns to interpret that state, not to shape it. The interesting question is therefore not how to optimise the network, but which physical or simulated system makes a good reservoir in the first place.

Be the readout layer

A reservoir holds a fading trace of what it has just seen. Watch the spikes travel through the network, then replay them in order — you are the trained readout, decoding a state you did not shape. Each round adds one step, until the memory outruns you.
